### [LORE & LANGUAGE PROFILE: AETHEL-VAEL]

**Description:** *Aethel-Vael* (literally "The Whispering Tongue") is an ancient, melodic language talked in Eldemere. It is characterized by soft consonants, fluid vowel transitions, and a structured, non-English grammatical order.

Use the following linguistic parameters, vocabulary database, and syntax rules to interpret, translate, or generate text in *Aethel-Vael*.

---

### 1. Grammatical Structure

*   **Sentence Order:** Subject-Object-Verb (SOV).
    *   *English:* "I see the star." $\rightarrow$ *Aethel-Vael:* "I star see."
*   **Adjectives:** Adjectives always follow the noun they modify.
    *   *English:* "The ancient tree." $\rightarrow$ *Aethel-Vael:* "The tree ancient."
*   **Pluralization:** Add the suffix **-is** to the end of a noun. If the noun ends in a vowel, drop the vowel before adding **-is**.
    *   *Example:* *Silva* (tree) $\rightarrow$ *Silvis* (trees).
*   **Possession (Genitive):** Add the suffix **-en** to the possessor noun.
    *   *Example:* *Aelis* (star) $\rightarrow$ *Aelisen* (of the star / star's).

---

### 2. Verb Conjugation & Tenses
Verbs are conjugated using prefixes attached directly to the verb root.

| Tense | Prefix | Example (Root: *Vael* - to speak) | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | *(None)* | *Vael* | speaks / speak |
| **Past** | **El-** | *Elvael* | spoke |
| **Future** | **Va-** | *Vavael* | will speak |
| **Imperative** | **Ki-** | *Kivael* | speak! (command) |

The rule for negation in 'Aethel-Vael' utilizes the word 'Moris' (shadow/darkness) to symbolize the absence or void of an action or quality.
Negation Rule: Place 'Moris' immediately before the verb or adjective being negated.
Examples:

1. Negative Verb: "I do not see the star."
   Structure: I [Ae] star [aelis] shadow [moris] see [viri].
   Aethel-Vael: Ae aelis moris viri.

2. Negative Adjective: "The tree is not ancient."
   Structure: The [Sae] tree [silva] shadow [moris] ancient [aeth].
   Aethel-Vael: Sae silva moris aeth.

    *   *Aethel-Vael:* **Ae aelis viri.**

To expand *Aethel-Vael* into complex sentence structures, the following conjunctions and syntactical rules are established. These rules maintain the core Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) order while allowing for the linkage of multiple ideas.

### 1. Conjunction Lexicon

The following terms are used to connect clauses or nouns:

*   'Lia' — And (used for addition or connection)
*   'Saelia' — But (used for contrast or contradiction)
*   'Vaela' — Or (used for choice or alternatives)
*   'Aelva' — Because / Since (used for causality)

---

### 2. Compound Sentence Rules

Compound sentences join two independent clauses. The conjunction is placed between the two complete SOV structures.

**Rule A: Independent Clauses**
Structure: [Subject 1] [Object 1] [Verb 1] + [Conjunction] + [Subject 2] [Object 2] [Verb 2].
*   English: "I see the star and he sees the tree."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Ae aelis viri lia vo silva viri.**

**Rule B: Subject Ellipsis**
If the subject is the same for both actions, the subject is omitted in the second clause to maintain melodic flow.
Structure: [Subject] [Object 1] [Verb 1] + [Conjunction] + [Object 2] [Verb 2].
*   English: "I see the star and (I) see the tree."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Ae aelis viri lia silva viri.**

**Rule C: Object Grouping**
If the verb is the same for multiple objects, the objects are linked by 'lia' before the verb.
Structure: [Subject] [Object 1] + [Lia] + [Object 2] + [Verb].
*   English: "I see the star and the tree."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Ae aelis lia silva viri.**

---

### 3. Contrast and Alternative Rules

'Saelia' (but) and 'Vaela' (or) follow the same placement as 'lia'.

**Example: Contrast (But)**
*   English: "I see the star but the forest is dark."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Ae aelis viri saelia silva kael.**

**Example: Alternative (Or)**
*   English: "I hold the stone or I hold the fire."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Ae thal oren vaela vhan oren.**

---

### 4. Complex Sentence Rules (Subordination)

When using 'aelva' (because/since), the dependent clause (the reason) must always precede the main clause (the result).

**Rule: Causal Precedence**
Structure: [Subordinate Subject] [Subordinate Object/Adj] [Subordinate Verb] + [Aelva] + [Main Subject] [Main Object] [Main Verb].
*   English: "Because the star is beautiful, I see it."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Aelis shari aelva ae vo viri.**
    *(Literal: Star beautiful because I it see.)*

**Example: Complex Negation**
*   English: "I do not see the star because the night is dark."
*   Aethel-Vael: **Moris kael aelva ae aelis moris viri.**
    *(Literal: Night dark because I star shadow see.)*
